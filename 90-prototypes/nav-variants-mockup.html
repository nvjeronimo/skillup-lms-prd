Introduction to the DMAIC methodology

Video · 3m 20s Not completed
AAdaptive single CTAOne right-side button that morphs by type and state. Action and navigation share one slot, one job at a time.
Strength: zero CTA competition, lowest cognitive load.   Cost: no way to skip ahead from the footer.
BDecoupled hierarchyPrev / Next stay persistent but quiet. The real task lives loud in the content area, not the footer.
Strength: always an escape route, minimal logic, lowest build risk.   Cost: two zones to scan.
CGated NextNext stays visible but disabled until the required action is done. Hover the disabled button for the reason.
Strength: enforces the path.   Cost: feels punitive on passive content — only gate what truly needs gating.
DAuto-advance on completionAction topics have no manual Next. Completing the task reveals Continue. The completion state becomes the navigation.
Strength: strong for assessments, momentum built in.   Cost: removes user control — keep Previous visible.
ENav out of the footerPrev / Next become arrows in the top bar. The footer is a pure action zone holding only the current task's CTA.
Strength: strongest separation of nav and action.   Cost: top arrows are easier to miss.
